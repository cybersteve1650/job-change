Interview preparation

Article: The essential Japan cyber security interview guide

Spend 20 minutes on the investor relations report

This is the highest return preparation you can do. Focus on:

  • How revenue is segmented, by product, service or geography.
  • Key technology investments, particularly anything AI, cloud or data related.
  • The forward outlook and stated risks. Security sits directly underneath this.

Do not disclose your current salary or expectations

Once you name a number, you cannot negotiate above it. I handle this conversation for you.

IF YOU ARE PUSHED

"My agent holds the full detail of my salary and benefits, and I would rather not give you a figure that turns out to be wrong."

Or give a wide band, for example 15 to 17 million JPY, which preserves room to move.

SPI test (Synthetic Personality Inventory)

Designed for graduate hiring, but some Japanese companies still run it on all hires late in the process. It covers language, maths and personality.

  • Every question is timed and you cannot skip.
  • Each section is separately timed. Overspend on one question and you lose the chance to answer others in that section.
  • English overview: japan-dev.com/blog/spi-test

Offer acceptance and resignation

Questions to ask in the offer meeting

  • How is the bonus calculated, and how much of it is company, team and personal performance?
  • Which months is the bonus paid?
  • In year one, do new joiners receive the full bonus or is it prorated to your start date?
  • What is the review cycle, and when would your first salary review fall?

Resignation checklist

0 of 10 done

Sign the offer letter and confirm your start date with the new employer.
Check your notice period in your employment rules (shugyo kisoku). One month is the common standard in Japan
Start dates are usually the 1st or 15th of the month, because onboarding runs in batches.
Decide your last working day and your last day on payroll. These are often different.
Unused annual leave is generally not paid out, so plan to use it before your final day.
Prepare a written list of open tasks and responsibilities for handover.
Book a meeting with your manager to resign in person. If they are away for more than a few days, speak to their manager instead.
Follow the meeting with a short email so the resignation is on record.
Prepare your response to a counter offer in advance.
Expect paper forms by post from Japanese employers before you start.
ON COUNTER OFFERS

A counter offer usually fixes the salary and none of the reasons you started looking. In our experience the large majority of people who accept one are back in the market within six months, having lost the offer they had and some credibility internally.
